# R3 — Academic & Technical Literature: Real-Time 1D Barcode Localization, Guidance, and Multi-Frame Decoding

Evidence file for a live barcode/product SCAN CORE architecture decision (mobile camera, moving hand, continuous video). Compiled 2026-09-03. Every bullet is tagged **[VERIFIED source]** (URL given, content confirmed from a fetched page or a search-engine-indexed excerpt of the actual page) or **[INFERENCE]** (my own reasoning/extrapolation, not directly sourced). Where a specific number or claim could not be independently confirmed despite attempts, this is stated explicitly rather than guessed.

**Methodology note on evidence quality:** Most primary PDFs in this literature (IEEE/Springer/ACM/author-site PDFs) could not be rendered as text by the fetch tooling available (binary/compressed-stream output only); HTML abstract pages on ResearchGate/Academia.edu returned HTTP 403 (bot-blocked). Where this happened, facts below were instead confirmed via (a) direct fetch of an HTML-rendered alternative (PMC, GitHub, Wikipedia, project pages), or (b) search-engine result snippets that quote/paraphrase the actual paper text with specific, internally-consistent detail (author names, venues, numbers) rather than generic summarization. Cases where neither was achievable are marked as open gaps, not filled with invented numbers.

## 1. Foundational Work

### Gallo & Manduchi — "Reading 1D Barcodes with Mobile Phones Using Deformable Templates" (IEEE TPAMI, 2011)

- Full citation confirmed: O. Gallo and R. Manduchi, IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, vol. 33, pp. 1834–1843, September 2011, DOI 10.1109/TPAMI.2010.229. [VERIFIED source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21173448/) [VERIFIED source](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Reading-1D-Barcodes-with-Mobile-Phones-Using-Gallo-Manduchi/45d668bf41f7e5188ee3fac72f80872ae89c52a7)
- Core method: bypasses binarization entirely — decodes directly from gray-level scanline intensity rather than thresholded bars/spaces, using **deformable barcode digit models in a maximum-likelihood setting**; the specific structure of these digit models permits **efficient integration over the continuous space of deformations**; **global optimization over all digits in the scanline is performed via dynamic programming** (a Viterbi-style pass across the digit sequence). [VERIFIED source](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~manduchi/papers/GalloWACV09.pdf) — description corroborated identically across the WACV'09 precursor and TPAMI'11 journal version.
- This is a **per-digit deformable template matched jointly across the scanline**, i.e. exactly the "per-digit deformable template matching on a scanline" method described in the brief. [VERIFIED source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224207512_Reading_1D_Barcodes_with_Mobile_Phones_Using_Deformable_Templates) (title/venue/DOI corroborated; abstract text itself blocked by 403)
- Robustness target explicitly stated as blur, noise, low resolution, and low-quality camera lenses — motivated by the observation that "virtually all existing algorithms perform some sort of binarization" and fail once that binarization step fails under these conditions. [VERIFIED source](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~manduchi/papers/GalloWACV09.pdf)
- Evaluated on "challenging UPC-A barcode images from five different databases," reporting outperformance of competing algorithms. [VERIFIED source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21173448/)
- **Gap, stated honestly:** the exact accuracy percentages and per-image decoding-time (ms) figures in the TPAMI 2011 results tables could not be extracted — the paper PDF (author site, IEEE, ResearchGate) rendered only as binary/compressed data to the fetch tool, and ResearchGate/PubMed abstract HTML was blocked or JS-only. No number is reported here that wasn't confirmed. [INFERENCE: numeric results exist in the paper but were not accessible through the tools available in this session]
- Related-work detail recovered (useful for comparing against Wachenfeld, below): Gallo & Manduchi characterize Wachenfeld et al.'s method as an "adaptive thresholding approach where they model different digits and find the combination that best explains the scanline," noting Wachenfeld "report[s] a very high accuracy" but does "not present any comparative test" against other methods. [VERIFIED source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224207512_Reading_1D_Barcodes_with_Mobile_Phones_Using_Deformable_Templates) (search-indexed excerpt)
- Precursor conference paper: Gallo & Manduchi, "Reading Challenging Barcodes with Cameras," WACV 2009 — same core deformable-template/DP method, described as the first publication of this approach; also released as UCSC Technical Report UCSC-SOE-09-26. [VERIFIED source](https://users.soe.ucsc.edu/~manduchi/papers/GalloWACV09.pdf) [VERIFIED source](https://tr.soe.ucsc.edu/sites/default/files/technical-reports/UCSC-SOE-09-26.pdf) [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2898212/)

### Wachenfeld, Terlunen & Jiang — "Robust Recognition of 1-D Barcodes Using Camera Phones" (ICPR 2008)

- Confirmed citation: ICPR 2008, pp. 1–4. [VERIFIED source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224374952_Robust_recognition_of_1-D_barcodes_using_camera_phones)
- Method (per Gallo & Manduchi's characterization, since the primary abstract was not directly renderable): an **adaptive-thresholding, per-digit modeling** approach — models candidate digits and selects the combination that "best explains" the observed scanline. [VERIFIED source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224207512_Reading_1D_Barcodes_with_Mobile_Phones_Using_Deformable_Templates) (search-indexed excerpt, cross-referenced from the citing paper)
- Created the dataset that became the field's standard benchmark: the **WWU Muenster BarcodeDB**, 1,055 EAN/UPC-A images captured with a **Nokia N95** phone at 640×480. [VERIFIED source](https://www.uni-muenster.de/PRIA/en/forschung/index.shtml)
- Reported accuracy "exceeding 90%" on public datasets per synthesized secondary description; **no head-to-head comparative test against other algorithms was included in the original paper** (per Gallo & Manduchi's own characterization above). [INFERENCE-adjacent: the >90% figure is a search-engine synthesis of the paper's claims, not a number I could read directly in the primary text — flagged as lower-confidence than the TPAMI/CRV numbers below, which came from a direct full-text fetch]

### Tekin & Coughlan (Smith-Kettlewell) — "An Algorithm Enabling Blind Users to Find and Read Barcodes" (WACV 2009)

- Confirmed: WACV 2009, Snowbird, Utah, December 2009; Smith-Kettlewell Eye Research Institute. [VERIFIED source](https://www.ski.org/publications/algorithm-enabling-blind-users-find-and-read-barcodes) [VERIFIED source](https://www.ski.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/wacv09-barcode.pdf)
- **Localization / detection-at-distance:** a fast algorithm based on a **cascade of filters that progressively rule out more and more regions of the image**, narrowing to barcode-like candidate regions before any expensive processing — the classic Viola-Jones-style cascade-rejection pattern applied to barcode-shaped bar/space texture rather than faces. [VERIFIED source](https://www.ski.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/wacv09-barcode.pdf) (search-indexed excerpt of the actual PDF text)
- **Guidance:** speech/audio feedback directs the user toward a detected-but-not-yet-decodable barcode, with directional instructions such as "closer." [VERIFIED source](https://www.ski.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/wacv09-barcode.pdf)
- **Evaluation:** tested with blindfolded (sighted) users, demonstrating feasibility for totally blind users — an unusually direct usability validation for a CV paper of this era. [VERIFIED source](https://www.ski.org/publications/algorithm-enabling-blind-users-find-and-read-barcodes)
- **Gap:** exact detection accuracy / fps numbers for this specific 2009 paper were not extractable (PDF binary issue); see the related CRV 2009 paper below, which gave hard numbers directly.

### Related finding (not in the original list, verified directly): Tekin & Coughlan — "A Bayesian Algorithm for Reading 1D Barcodes" (CRV 2009)

This is a distinct, closely related Tekin/Coughlan paper that happened to be fully readable (PMC full text), so it is reported here as a bonus data point directly answering the brief's "multi-hypothesis tracking with checksum pruning" question.

- Confirmed: Canadian Conference on Computer and Robot Vision (CRV) 2009. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859730/)
- Method: a **Bayesian deformable-template model operating on scanlines**, with two variants — **Model 1** restricts candidate edges to local intensity-gradient extrema and uses the **Viterbi algorithm** for inference; **Model 2** allows edges anywhere on a pixel lattice (so it can represent *missing* edges) and uses **belief propagation** to get marginal probabilities per edge/digit. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859730/)
- Directly relevant to multi-hypothesis + checksum pruning: the framework **incorporates the UPC-A checksum constraint to detect and correct single-digit errors** — i.e., checksum-gated hypothesis selection over a probabilistic decode, on a single scanline. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859730/)
- **Hard numbers** (rare in this literature): tested on 79 manually segmented images split into "clean" (44) and "hard" (35). Clean set: **42/44 correct** vs. 39/44 and 13/44 for two commercial reference readers. Hard set: **2/35 correct** vs. **0/35** for both commercial readers. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859730/)
- **Timing — important caveat for the decision:** "a few tens of seconds" per image for Model 1, "many tens of seconds" for Model 2, in **unoptimized MATLAB**. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859730/) — this is a research-grade proof of accuracy on hard cases, explicitly **not** a real-time result; any production use would require re-implementation for speed.
- Datasets: Nokia N95, Nikon Coolpix 4300, and internet images; a publicly annotated dataset was posted online (specific current URL not confirmed / likely stale). [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859730/)

### BLaDE — Barcode Localization and Decoding Engine (Tekin & Coughlan, 2012/2013)

- Confirmed as an Android app (plus Linux desktop build) from the Smith-Kettlewell Coughlan Lab, journal-length evaluation published as "S-K Smartphone Barcode Reader for the Blind." [VERIFIED source](https://www.ski.org/project/blade/) [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4288446/)
- **Localization:** analyzes **multiple video frames per second**; explicitly works "even when only part of the barcode is visible in the image, or when the barcode is too far away from the camera to be read" — i.e., it detects-at-a-distance *before* attempting to decode, same as the WACV'09 cascade-filter precursor. Barcodes can be at **any orientation**, no horizontal/vertical alignment required. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4288446/)
- **Guidance mechanism (fully specified, high value for the design decision):** two independent audio channels —
  - **Volume** encodes barcode size in the image (i.e., apparent distance): louder = closer to the correct scanning distance.
  - **Tone continuity** (stuttered → continuous) encodes centering/alignment quality: continuous tone = well-centered.
  - Users are explicitly trained to use the two cues in sequence: **first** translate the camera to get a continuous tone (centering), **then** adjust distance to maximize volume. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4288446/)
  - This is a clean, two-axis decomposition of the guidance problem (alignment vs. distance) that is directly reusable as a design pattern regardless of modality (audio → could map to a visual reticle + a distance meter).
- **User study numbers:** 5 visually-impaired participants, 90 trials total. **With feedback: 41/50 successes (82%), median time 41 s.** **Without feedback: 26/40 successes (65%), median time 68 s.** Statistical test gave **p = 0.056** — a trend favoring audio feedback, not quite significant at the usual threshold (small-N study). [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4288446/)
- **Multi-frame decode fusion — explicit gap:** the accessible text describes frame-rate *localization* clearly but **does not describe the specific mechanism used to fuse decode evidence across multiple frames or scanlines once a barcode is centered** (e.g., whether it's per-decode majority vote, per-digit accumulation, or single-frame-when-quality-is-good-enough). This is stated as a gap rather than inferred. [INFERENCE: some fusion almost certainly exists given "evidence accumulation across frames" is core to the product's usability, but the mechanism is not described in the sections of the source I could access]
- **Source code released as open source**, confirmed license location: originally `http://legacy.ski.org/Rehab/Coughlan_lab/BLaDE/`, later moved to **SourceForge, `sourceforge.net/p/sk-blade`**, described as free to use or modify for any purpose. [VERIFIED source](https://www.ski.org/project/blade/) — the exact SPDX license identifier (e.g. BSD/GPL/MIT) was not stated in the fetched text; only "open source... free of charge" is confirmed.

### Zamberletti, Gallo & Albertini — "Robust Angle Invariant 1D Barcode Detection" (ACPR 2013)

## 2. Multi-Frame / Temporal Decoding (core question)

This is the thinnest part of the literature relative to how central it is to the architecture decision. Findings:

- **(a) Per-digit probabilistic accumulation, single-scanline:** proven feasible by Tekin & Coughlan's CRV 2009 Bayesian model — belief propagation over a pixel-lattice edge model, with checksum-constrained correction of single-digit errors. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2859730/) This is a *within-image* multi-hypothesis system; I found **no direct academic evidence that this specific model was extended across multiple video frames**, only that its digit-level probabilistic representation is exactly the kind of representation a multi-frame accumulator would need per frame.
- **(b) Frame registration + scanline averaging/SR:** the clearest engineering (not peer-reviewed) precedent is **OpenCV's barcode module**, which can optionally load a **super-resolution CNN** (`sr.caffemodel`/`sr.prototxt`) originally built for the **WeChat QR code project** (Tencent), which pairs a detection CNN with a "zoom in" super-resolution CNN applied to small barcode crops before decoding, with different SR scale factors chosen by initial crop size. [VERIFIED source](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d6/d25/tutorial_barcode_detect_and_decode.html) [VERIFIED source](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d5/d04/classcv_1_1wechat__qrcode_1_1WeChatQRCode.html) **Important caveat:** this SR model, as documented, is applied to a **single cropped image**, not fused across multiple video frames — it is a resolution-enhancement step, not a temporal-fusion step. I found **no academic paper** (arXiv or otherwise) describing this SR model's training/architecture in a citable, peer-reviewed form; its provenance is an engineering artifact release (GitHub `WeChatCV/opencv_3rdparty`), not a paper. [VERIFIED source](https://github.com/WeChatCV/opencv_3rdparty/tree/wechat_qrcode) Flagging this explicitly since the brief asked to verify whether this SR model has an academic origin — **it does not appear to**, based on available search.
- **(c) Multi-hypothesis tracking with checksum pruning:** the EAN/UPC mod-10 checksum (Section 6) is the standard mechanism the whole field leans on for pruning bad hypotheses — every foundational paper above (Gallo/Manduchi, Tekin/Coughlan CRV'09) explicitly uses the checksum to accept/reject/correct single-digit hypotheses. No paper found that extends this into an explicit multi-frame Kalman/particle-filter-style tracker over digit hypotheses across frames — this appears to be an open/unaddressed design space in the *academic* literature specifically for 1D barcodes (contrast with video OCR, Section 5, where multi-frame hypothesis tracking is well studied).
- **(d) Majority voting over independent decodes:** two lines of evidence —
  - *Academic:* Adelmann/Langheinrich/Flörkemeier (2006) is characterized in the field as an early multi-frame camera-phone system, but I could not confirm its voting mechanism or accuracy numbers directly (see Section 1 gap). [VERIFIED source](https://vs.inf.ethz.ch/publ/papers/barcodes2006.pdf)
  - *Patent/industrial prior art (not peer-reviewed, but directly on-point and worth citing as existence-proof):* barcode-scanner patents describe **"signal-aided voting"** — mapping a per-scanline **data-quality map** (marking symbols erased by glare, shadow, reflection, dirt, occlusion, or package folding) and using that quality map to **exclude unreliable symbols from a symbol-wise vote**, combined with an **alignment step that registers symbols across scanlines before voting** (to correct for warping/rotation/perspective misalignment between scanlines). The explicit design goal stated in this prior art is recovering a **complete barcode ID even when no single scanline/decode recovered all digits** — i.e., exactly the "progressive multi-frame reconstruction" scenario in question. [VERIFIED source](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6454168) (USPTO patent full text; **this is patent literature, not an academic paper** — flagged as such per the brief's request for what's "proven to work," since a granted, implemented patent is real-world existence-proof even though it isn't peer-reviewed research)
- **How alignment across frames/scanlines is done, per the above:** guard-pattern/module-boundary registration is implicit (scanlines are aligned to each other, then symbols — i.e. decoded digits at fixed module positions — are compared position-by-position before voting). No academic paper was found that names or formalizes "guard anchoring" as a term of art for this, but the *mechanism* it must use (fixing the guard-pattern positions, then indexing modules/digits relative to them) is the only geometrically sensible way to do this, and is consistent with how every decoder (open-source or academic) locates digit boundaries in the first place. [INFERENCE, grounded in the EAN-13 structural facts in Section 6 plus the patent-described alignment step above]
- **Quantified false-positive risk of stitching partial reads:** **no source found** — academic or patent — that quantifies the false-positive/false-accept rate of a system that stitches together partial decodes across frames or scanlines. This is a genuine, notable gap in the literature. The only quantitative backstop available is the EAN-13 checksum's known error-detection profile (Section 6): it catches all single-digit substitutions and all adjacent transpositions except when the two digits differ by exactly 5 — meaning a naive stitching scheme that assembles digits from different frames independently, gated only by the mod-10 checksum, has a **known, non-zero, but unquantified-in-the-literature residual acceptance rate for wrong digit combinations**, concentrated in the "differ-by-5" transposition case. [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number) + [INFERENCE for how this bears on multi-frame stitching specifically]
- **Barcode deblurring / blind deconvolution — classics, both verified directly:**
  - **Esedoglu (2004), "Blind deconvolution of bar code signals,"** *Inverse Problems* 20:121–135. Develops a **total-variation-based variational model** for recovering a clean 1D barcode signal from one convolved with an *unknown* blur kernel plus noise — harder than standard deblurring because the kernel itself is unknown, but tractable because barcode signals have a very restricted, known structural form (alternating bars/spaces) that constrains the joint estimation of signal + kernel. [VERIFIED source](https://dept.math.lsa.umich.edu/~esedoglu/Papers_Preprints/barcodes_esedoglu.pdf) [VERIFIED source](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0266-5611/20/1/007) — year and journal confirmed as requested (2004, not misremembered).
  - **Iwen, Santosa & Ward (2013), "A symbol-based algorithm for decoding bar codes,"** SIAM Journal on Imaging Sciences 6(1):56–77. [VERIFIED source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7009) Method: treats the known **UPC symbology as a dictionary** and searches for a **sparse representation** of the observed (laser-scanner) signal with respect to that dictionary, which sharply **reduces the degrees of freedom** relative to generic signal reconstruction; provides theoretical guarantees and demonstrates robustness/insensitivity to both **noise** and **imprecise/unknown scanner parameters**. [VERIFIED source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7009) This is conceptually the closest classical-literature analogue to "reconstruct one barcode from many degraded partial reads" — same idea (constrain reconstruction using the known symbol dictionary/checksum structure), applied to a single degraded 1D signal rather than multiple video frames, but directly portable to a multi-frame setting where each frame contributes a noisy partial signal to the same sparse-recovery problem. [INFERENCE for the multi-frame extension — not claimed by the paper itself]
- **Deep learning barcode decoding as sequence labeling (CTC/CRNN) — explicitly checked, mostly not found as real, on-point work:** general CRNN+CTC sequence-recognition architecture (from scene-text/OCR literature) is well established as a technique family. [VERIFIED source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04256) However, an extensive search for a **peer-reviewed paper applying CTC/CRNN specifically to *decode* 1D barcode digit sequences** (as opposed to *detect/localize* the barcode region) did not surface one. The deep-learning barcode literature found in this session (Hansen et al. 2017; Zharkov & Zagaynov 2019; the BarBeR-listed methods; InventBar/ParcelBar's YOLO/EfficientDet/RetinaNet/Faster R-CNN comparisons) is **overwhelmingly about detection/localization**, with decoding still handed off to a classical decoder (e.g., **pyzbar**, per BarBeR's own pipeline — see Section 3). [VERIFIED source](https://github.com/Henvezz95/BarBeR) **This is itself an important finding for the architecture decision: "deep sequence-labeling barcode decoder" does not appear to be a mature, citable, off-the-shelf academic technique as of this search — detection has been deep-learned; decoding largely has not.** [INFERENCE drawn from the consistent absence across a comprehensive search, not a positive claim that no such paper exists anywhere]

### BarBeR — Barcode Benchmark Repository (Vezzali, Bolelli, Santi, Grana)

- Confirmed real and actively maintained: **ICPR 2024** paper ("BarBeR: A Barcode Benchmarking Repository"), a follow-up **RRPR 2024** reproducibility-notes paper, and a **2025 journal article in *Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence*** (titled as a "state-of-the-art review and benchmarking of barcode localization methods"). Authors: E. Vezzali, F. Bolelli, S. Santi (Datalogic S.p.A.), C. Grana — University of Modena and Reggio Emilia + Datalogic. [VERIFIED source](https://github.com/Henvezz95/BarBeR) [VERIFIED source](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-78447-7_13) [VERIFIED source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0952197625002593)
- **Dataset composition:** **8,748 annotated barcode images**, resolutions ranging from **200×141 to 5,984×3,376**, captured across varied devices/lighting (including under- and over-exposed conditions). [VERIFIED source](https://iris.unimo.it/retrieve/5871f428-02f0-4e54-9667-36e6ec9f1124/2024icpr_barber.pdf) (search-indexed excerpt)

### InventBar and ParcelBar (from "1D Barcode Detection: Novel Benchmark Datasets and Comprehensive Comparison of Deep Convolutional Neural Network Approaches," MDPI Sensors 2022)

Fully verified via a direct full-text fetch (PMC), the strongest-sourced dataset entry in this report:

- **InventBar:** 527 images of consumer goods, **527 barcode instances**, 86.14% "large" barcodes relative to image size. [VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9696533/)

[VERIFIED source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9696533/)
- Also indexed as PubMed 36433385; datasets publicly available on Kaggle. [VERIFIED source](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/chommaphatma/inventbar-and-parcelbar-benchmarkbarcodedatasets)

Decoding itself, once localized, is still generally handed to a classical decoder (pyzbar/ZBar/ZXing) rather than a learned decoder. ## 4. Guidance / Distance Estimation

- **px/module decodability thresholds — found, but from technical/industry rather than peer-reviewed academic sources; reported with that caveat.** Consistent figures across independent industry technical references: for 2D codes, **~8 px/module is the target, ~6 px/module the marginal lower bound**, with "**at least 2 pixels per module**" as a commonly cited practical baseline; for 1D barcodes, roughly **10 px per bar minimum**, with narrower-element symbologies (e.g. Code 39) needing on the order of **12–24 px per character** in practice. [VERIFIED source](https://www.bardecode.com/en1/minimum-width-and-height-for-barcodes-in-images/) [VERIFIED source](https://commonlands.com/blogs/technical/barcode-reading-machine-vision) One source states that images with **pixels-per-element (PPE) below 1** are effectively undecodable by conventional means, that **1–2 px/module is "sometimes possible,"** and that specialized reconstruction (oversampled-signal techniques, conceptually the same family as Iwen/Esedoglu's academic work above) can push below 1 px/module. [VERIFIED source](https://www.bardecode.com/en1/minimum-width-and-height-for-barcodes-in-images/) — **the brief's own reference anchor of "~1.5–2 px/module" sits squarely inside this range**, but I could not trace it to a specific peer-reviewed paper; treat it as an industry-consensus practical threshold rather than an academically-derived one.
- **Tilt/perspective estimation from a barcode quad:** I found this described only as a **component within larger patented/commercial systems**, not as a standalone academic contribution: locate the barcode's bounding quadrilateral, and if the slope of its sides exceeds a threshold, trigger perspective correction (interpolating scanlines via a Bresenham-style line-walk on the corrected geometry); separately, phone **accelerometers** are used to directly estimate camera roll/pitch as a complementary tilt signal. [VERIFIED source](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8881984) — again, **patent literature, not academic**, flagged accordingly. Zamberletti et al.'s angle-invariant detector (Section 1) produces an angle estimate as a byproduct of detection, but the paper frames this as a detection-robustness contribution, not an explicit "tell the user to rotate the phone" guidance system. [INFERENCE bridging the two]
- **HCI response-time thresholds — solidly verified, classic reference:** Jakob Nielsen's three response-time limits (from *Usability Engineering*, 1993, popularized further via Nielsen Norman Group): **0.1 s** — the limit for feeling that the system is responding instantaneously / direct manipulation; **1.0 s** — the limit for the user's flow of thought to stay uninterrupted (delay is noticed, but no special feedback needed below this); **10 s** — the limit for keeping the user's attention on the task at all before they mentally disengage. [VERIFIED source](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/) These map directly onto scan-guidance UI budgets: guidance-signal updates (reticle/tone changes) should land inside the **0.1 s** budget to feel like direct manipulation of the camera; a full decode taking up to **~1 s** will still feel responsive; anything approaching **10 s** risks the user giving up on the scan attempt entirely.
- **Haptic/visual confirmation studies specific to barcode scanning:** **no dedicated academic study found.** This is a genuine gap — the guidance-modality literature located here (BLaDE's audio volume+tone design, Section 1) is the closest evidence of a *validated* confirmation-signal design, but it is audio-only and aimed at blind/low-vision users, not a general haptic-vs-visual confirmation comparison. [Explicit gap, not filled with invented sources]

## 5. Video OCR / Text Fusion

- **Row-Based Multiple Frame Integration (RB-MFI)** — "Efficient video text recognition using multiple frame integration": combines the **most accurate rows** of OCR text output extracted independently from multiple frames, rather than voting whole-word outputs; reported **character recognition rates up to 98%**, a substantial gain over single-frame OCR on the same low-quality video text. [VERIFIED source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/3970403_Efficient_video_text_recognition_using_multiple_frame_integration) (search-indexed excerpt; original is an IEEE conference paper, document ID 1039971) [VERIFIED source](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1039971/similar) This is a directly relevant precedent for "per-module/per-character probabilistic accumulation" in the barcode case — same idea (best-evidence-per-position fusion) applied at the character-row level instead of the digit-module level.
- **ROVER — Recognizer Output Voting Error Reduction (Fiscus, 1997, NIST):** the canonical **alignment + voting** architecture for fusing multiple independent noisy sequence-decodes into one better sequence. Originally built for multi-system ASR fusion; the same alignment/voting architecture has since been adapted to **OCR consensus-sequence voting** (confirmed via multiple derivative papers citing and extending it, e.g. "LV-ROVER: Lexicon Verified Recognizer Output Voting Error Reduction" and later Maltese-OCR work using a multi-stream ROVER variant). [VERIFIED source](https://www.nist.gov/publications/post-processing-system-yield-reduced-word-error-rates-recognizer-output-voting-error) [VERIFIED source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.07432) Mechanism: an **alignment module** first time-aligns the independent output sequences against each other, then a **voting module** picks, position by position, the token with the most support across sequences. This is architecturally the most directly transferable precedent in this whole report for "align multiple independent frame-decodes, then vote digit-by-digit" — it is a proven, decades-old, widely-reused pattern, just never (per this search) applied specifically to 1D barcodes.
- **Merino-Gracia & Mirmehdi — real-time text tracking (IET Computer Vision, 2014; wearable variant with Lenc)**: MSER-based text detection at **~14 fps on 640×480 images**; uses a bank of **Unscented Kalman Filters (UKF)** to maintain each tracked text region's identity over time and continuously warp/track its **homography into a fronto-parallel view** as the camera or text moves — i.e., persistent per-instance tracking + on-the-fly perspective rectification, running in real time on a handheld/wearable rig, with "quick failure recovery." [VERIFIED source](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=0JFjnJMAAAAJ&hl=es) (paper existence/venue/method corroborated via search-indexed description; direct HTML abstract not independently re-confirmed beyond the search synthesis) This is the closest video-OCR analogue to what a barcode system would need for *continuous tracking while the guided-scan hand is moving* — not fusing decoded characters per se, but keeping a stable rectified view of the target region across frames, which is a prerequisite for any per-frame fusion to align cleanly.
- **Edit-distance-based fusion:** confirmed as a general technique used across this literature — recognized text strings from different frames are merged using **edit distance** to handle small character-level disagreements between otherwise-matching frame decodes, rather than requiring byte-exact agreement before voting. [VERIFIED source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258813785_A_research_on_Video_text_tracking_and_recognition) (search-indexed synthesis across multiple video-text-recognition surveys, not a single named paper)

## 6. EAN-13 Structure Facts (verified against GS1-adjacent and reference sources)

All core structural facts cross-verified against Wikipedia's International Article Number page (itself sourced from GS1 General Specifications) and GS1 Ireland's official size-guide page; arithmetic cross-checked for internal consistency.

- **Total symbol length: 95 modules.** Breakdown: 3 (start guard) + 42 (left-half, 6 digits × 7 modules) + 5 (center guard) + 42 (right-half, 6 digits × 7 modules) + 3 (end guard) = 95. [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number) — Note: one vendor documentation page (Accusoft) states "113 modules," which is inconsistent with this arithmetic and with every other source checked; **treat that figure as an error in that vendor's docs**, not as an alternative valid figure. [VERIFIED source, flagged as contradicted](https://help.accusoft.com/BarcodeXpress/v13.7/BxLinuxCC/ean-13.html)
- **Guard patterns:** start = **101**, center = **01010**, end = **101** (bar=1, space=0). [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number)
- **Modules per digit: 7** (each digit = 2 bars + 2 spaces, each 1–4 modules wide, summing to 7). [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number) [VERIFIED source](https://help.accusoft.com/BarcodeXpress/v13.7/BxLinuxCC/ean-13.html)
- **First digit encoding:** not directly encoded as its own symbol — instead carried implicitly via the **parity pattern (L-code/G-code odd/even parity) sequence across the left-hand six digits**; the right-hand six digits always use R-codes (even parity). A lookup table maps each of the 10 possible parity sequences to the implied first digit (e.g., all-L ⇒ first digit 0). [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number)
- **Checksum:** mod-10 weighted sum, alternating weights of **3 and 1** across the 12 data digits (positions counted appropriately from the right), check digit is whatever makes the total a multiple of 10. [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number)
- **Error-detection power:** detects **all single-digit substitution errors**; detects **adjacent-digit transpositions except when the two transposed digits differ by exactly 5** (e.g., swapping a 3 and an 8) — because the alternating ±weight structure makes those two cases produce an identical checksum. [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number) [VERIFIED source, worked example with digits "38"→"83"](https://medium.com/@michael.harges/implementing-the-upc-ean-check-digit-algorithm-in-c-e99c26a540c8) — one secondary source phrases the general transposition-detection rate as "~90% of all two-digit transposition errors," consistent with the "except differ-by-5" rule (since digit pairs differing by exactly 5 are a small, specific subset of all possible transpositions). [VERIFIED source](https://medium.com/@michael.harges/implementing-the-upc-ean-check-digit-algorithm-in-c-e99c26a540c8)
- **Quiet zone:** minimum **11 modules (3.63 mm at nominal size)** on the left, **7 modules (2.31 mm)** on the right. [VERIFIED source](https://www.gs1ie.org/standards/data-carriers/barcodes/ean-13/) (A separate vendor page gives a looser general rule of "at least 10 narrow bars," roughly consistent in order of magnitude.) [VERIFIED source](https://help.accusoft.com/BarcodeXpress/v13.7/BxLinuxCC/ean-13.html)
- **Human-readable digit layout: 1 + 6 + 6** — the first digit printed to the left of the symbol, then two groups of six digits under the left and right halves respectively. [VERIFIED source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Article_Number)
- **Nominal X-dimension: 0.330 mm.** [VERIFIED source](https://www.gs1ie.org/standards/data-carriers/barcodes/ean-13/)
- **Nominal total symbol dimensions: 37.29 mm × 27.85 mm** (width × height, at 100% magnification; GS1 permits 80–200% magnification in practice). [VERIFIED source](https://www.gs1ie.org/standards/data-carriers/barcodes/ean-13/) — this matches the brief's requested "nominal symbol width 37.29 mm" figure exactly.
